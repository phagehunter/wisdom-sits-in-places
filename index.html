Wisdom Sits in Places

A study companion to Keith H. Basso's ethnography of landscape and language among the Western Apache, by Curtis Hoffmann

This single-page study aid covers Wisdom Sits in Places: Landscape and Language Among the Western Apache (University of New Mexico Press, 1996), from the preface through all four essays to the closing meditation on sense of place. A star marks the passages, places and terms the book keeps returning to, the ones worth committing to memory.

A section slider (Preface to Coda) and group toggles re-draw the filtered views, and a playback control animates the book section by section. Clicking any place, person, term or condition opens the commentary panel with notes and page citations, each linking into the Guide tab of original per-section synopses.

The six views

  • Study Guide. The landing sheet: the book in one idea, the four Apache teachers and the lesson each teaches, the four Chapter 1 places with toponym, naming, story and lesson, the three conditions of mind, the speech-genre table, every starred term and a per-chapter nutshell.
  • The Named Land. All eighteen named places as a schematic country, ring color by naming layer (descriptive, clan or commemorative), gold stars on the places that carry the most weight. Deliberately not a map.
  • People and Places. A force-directed graph in which people, places, story figures and cited scholars share one network. Red dashed edges mark a story aimed at someone.
  • Key Terms. The book's vocabulary as a concept map: Basso's analytic terms, the Western Apache words the argument turns on, and the theory he borrows. A gold ring marks the essential vocabulary.
  • Trail of Wisdom. Chapter 4 as a study sheet: smoothness, resilience and steadiness of mind, each with its metaphor and consequences, then how wisdom is acquired and how its presence or absence is detected.
  • Narrative Gravity. A stream graph of what each section attends to (places, language, wisdom, the ancestral past, method), with a teacher strip under the axis.

Two decisions are stated plainly: nothing here locates a place, because Basso published under restrictions he said would never be violated, and no diacritics are invented, so toponyms follow his printed spellings.
